01 — The object

What it is

An inline four-cylinder engine modeled in SolidWorks 2026 — cylinder block, pistons, crankshaft and valvetrain, each built as its own component and mated individually rather than shortcut into a single body.

What separates this from a model that merely looks like an engine is that the internal components are configured to interact mechanically. Turn the crank and the pistons follow through their real travel. That constraint is what made it worth building: it forced every clearance and every relationship to be correct rather than approximately correct, and it is the reason the four-stroke cycle reads as kinematics instead of as a picture.

02 — The build

How I made it

Build the thing that sets the dimensions first, then work outward. In an engine that means the block.

  1. 01

    Start from the bore spacing

    The block fixes everything downstream — bore centres, deck height, main journal line. Get those wrong and every part after it inherits the error, so they get modeled and checked before anything else exists.

    Datum
  2. 02

    Model the reciprocating set

    Piston, pin, rod and journal as separate parts with the clearances they actually need, so nothing is press-fit by accident when the assembly closes up.

    Part modeling
  3. 03

    Constrain motion, not pose

    Mates that deliberately leave a degree of freedom, so the crank rotates and the pistons travel. Over-constraining is the instinct that makes an assembly look finished and hides every mistake in it.

    Kinematics
  4. 04

    Phase the valvetrain

    Cam and valves timed against crank position so intake, compression, power and exhaust land where they should across all four cylinders rather than each one being right in isolation.

    Timing
03 — The takeaway

How it grew me

A static model only has to look right once. A moving one has to be right at every angle.

The turbofan taught me to plan an assembly. This one taught me to leave it alone. My first instinct was to lock every part down until nothing could move — which feels like rigour and is actually the opposite, because a fully constrained assembly cannot tell you when two parts would collide. Deliberately leaving the crank free was the moment the model started checking my work instead of just displaying it.

It also gave me a much more physical understanding of the four-stroke cycle than any diagram had. You cannot phase a valvetrain you do not actually understand.
