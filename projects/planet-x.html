01 — The object

What it is

Since 2025, NASA's SPHEREx mission has been mapping the entire sky every six months in 102 bands of near-infrared light, returning images of more than a billion objects. Comets, asteroids, stars, brown dwarfs and even new planets reveal themselves by shifting position between images.

The challenge is to make that visible to anyone. We are building a public-facing web tool that displays SPHEREx sky imagery and lets a visitor see, quickly and without a research account, how celestial objects move and change over time. I'm leading a team of three; our submission date is 15 November 2026.

02 — The build

How I made it

Still in progress. This is the plan we are working to, and the part we are on.

  1. 01

    Work out what actually moves

    Fixed stars hold position between passes; the interesting objects do not. Everything the tool does hangs off making that difference legible in the first few seconds someone looks at it.

    Framing
  2. 02

    Get the imagery in and registered

    Pulling sky tiles for a given region across successive passes and aligning them so the same patch of sky lines up frame to frame. Without registration, everything looks like it moved.

    In progress
  3. 03

    Make it scrub

    A timeline the visitor drags. Same patch of sky, pass by pass, so movement reads as movement rather than as two pictures you have to mentally subtract.

    Interaction
  4. 04

    Make it genuinely public

    No install, no account, no jargon wall. Someone who has never heard of SPHEREx should find something moving inside a minute.

    The goal
03 — The takeaway

How it grew me

I have built assemblies alone before. Splitting one build three ways is a different problem, and most of it is not technical.

This is the first project where my job is not only to make the thing. Scoping it so three people can work in parallel, deciding what we cut when November gets close, and keeping everyone pointed at the same definition of done — that is the part I am actually learning from.
