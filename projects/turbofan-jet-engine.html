01 — The object

What it is

A complete turbofan jet engine assembly, modeled in SolidWorks 2026 from twenty-three individually built parts, grouped into the four stages a real engine actually has: fan, compressor, combustion, turbine.

The point was never a pretty render. It was to build something whose interfaces hold — where every mate corresponds to a real relationship between two parts — and then to cut one third of it away so somebody who has never opened SolidWorks can look at it and see how thrust gets made. A section cut is the honest test of an assembly: if the internals were faked, the cut plane exposes it immediately.

02 — The build

How I made it

Four passes. The order mattered more than any single feature — get it wrong and the rebuild cost compounds.

  1. 01

    Split it into stages

    Fan, compressor, combustor, turbine — each as its own subassembly, so mates stay local and the feature tree stays readable at part twenty-three instead of part six.

    Structure
  2. 02

    Model to the interface

    Every part dimensioned against the face it actually meets, not against the origin. Blade roots to the disc, casing to the flange, shaft to the bearing seat.

    Part modeling
  3. 03

    Mate stage by stage

    Coordinating mates across four stage interfaces, one boundary at a time, so interference shows up while it is still cheap to fix rather than at final assembly.

    Assembly
  4. 04

    Cut it open cleanly

    A precision section across one third of the assembly, placed to expose the core without severing the relationships that hold the remaining two thirds together.

    Section view
03 — The takeaway

How it grew me

Modeling the parts was the easy half. Deciding what order to build them in was the engineering.

I rebuilt the compressor stage twice because I mated it to the wrong reference early and every downstream part inherited the mistake. That is a lesson you only learn by paying for it: in a twenty-three part assembly, a bad decision on part four is not a part-four problem, it is a parts-five-through-twenty-three problem. Now I plan the mate scheme on paper before I open a sketch.

The section cut taught me the other half — that a model is a communication tool, and an assembly nobody can read is only half finished.
