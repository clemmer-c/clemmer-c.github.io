01 — The object

What it is

A full-service lawn care business I started in April 2021 and still run: mowing, edging, trimming, aerating, overseeding and fertilizing for five ongoing residential and commercial clients, on equipment I operate and maintain myself.

It belongs on an engineering portfolio for one reason. Every other project here shows I can model a system. This one shows I can run one — under weather, under wear, under a client who expects the same result every visit whether or not I feel like delivering it.

02 — The build

How I made it

Nobody hands you the process for this. Four things I worked out the expensive way.

  1. 01

    Price the route, not the lawn

    The cost that actually bites is travel between properties, not time on any one of them. Pricing per-lawn looked fair and quietly lost money on the outliers.

    Costing
  2. 02

    Service equipment before it fails

    A mower that dies mid-route costs a week of trust, not an afternoon. Blades, belts and filters on a schedule is cheaper than any single emergency.

    Maintenance
  3. 03

    Schedule to growth and weather

    Grass does not grow on a calendar. Reading the week ahead and moving visits before the client has to ask is most of what separates a service from a chore.

    Scheduling
  4. 04

    Stay ahead of the rules

    Tracking the regulations that apply to the work so nothing lands on a client as a surprise. Boring, and the reason five clients are still five clients.

    Compliance
03 — The takeaway

How it grew me

An assembly tells you immediately when it is wrong. A business takes a season to tell you, and by then you have already paid.

Running this taught me the half of engineering that CAD cannot: that a system has to survive contact with real conditions and real people, and that the feedback loop is slow and expensive. It made me much more careful about decisions I cannot easily reverse — which is exactly the instinct that stopped me over-constraining the inline four.

It also taught me to finish. Four years of showing up on a Saturday morning for a lawn nobody is inspecting is a different skill from finishing a model that is fun to build.
